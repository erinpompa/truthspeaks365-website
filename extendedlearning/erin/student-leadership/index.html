The WOOP Method for Student Leadership

A ready-to-run guide for leading your student leadership group after any event. Reflection questions, a lesson plan you can adjust to fit your time, and a printable worksheet. You lead it, they do the thinking.

Start here

Set aside 30 to 40 minutes

After your students get back from an event, conference, or assembly, block out a class period or 30 to 40 minutes within a day or two. You'll lead it. Your students do the thinking. Everything you need is on this page.

Before you start

  • Make a list of every session, workshop, and speaker your students saw.
  • Print one WOOP worksheet per student (bottom of this page).
  • Set up the room so students can move into small groups.
  • Grab pens, sticky notes, and chart paper.

At a glance

Feel free to adapt this to fit your time.

  • 3 min: Open with a quick check-in
  • 7 min: Students reflect and pick their ONE thing
  • 8 min: WOOP for themselves
  • 8 min: Share in groups and pick a school focus
  • 10 min: WOOP for the school or group
  • 4 min: Students share their plans with a partner

Part 1

Reflection questions

These go with Step 1 of the lesson. The lesson gives you three starter questions. Use these to swap in or go deeper. Pick two or three, not all of them. You can also use them on their own anytime, like a quick meeting opener.

Start with them

Ask your students:

  • What from this event is still sitting with you?
  • What left an imprint on you? Not your favorite part, the part you keep thinking about.
  • What's your black dot right now? The thing you can't stop staring at.
  • What part of it is in your control? What part isn't?
  • Where's your white space? Where can you still show up?
  • What do you need to stop carrying?
  • When was the last time you did something just to refill your own cup?

Then the school or group

Ask your students:

  • What's one thing you wish was different about our school?
  • Who in our school might feel unseen right now?
  • What's one thing our group does well that we should do more of?
  • What gets in the way of us following through?
  • If you could change ONE thing in the next three months, what would it be?
  • What would students say about our group if we weren't in the room?

About the event

Ask your students:

  • Which session or speaker is still in your head? Why that one?
  • What's one idea you heard that could actually work here?
  • Did anything challenge you or make you uncomfortable? What was it?
  • What's something you want to try in the next 30 days?

Going deeper

For when your group is ready:

  • What's the story you tell yourself when things get hard? Is it true?
  • What's something you've had to accept that made you stronger?
  • What are you grateful for right now, even in the middle of something hard?
  • What kind of leader do you want people to remember you as?
Part 1, continued

Ways to run it

Pick a format that fits your group. Some students think out loud, some need quiet first. Mix it up.

Quiet journal

Give students two to three minutes of silent writing before anyone talks. That way everyone has something to say when it's time to share.

Turn and talk

Pair students up, one minute each. Then call on a few pairs to share what their partner said, not what they said.

Four corners

Label the corners Impactful, Challenge, Growth, Curiosity. Have students walk to the one that fits what stuck with them, then talk with their corner.

Sticky note gallery walk

Post questions on chart paper around the room. Have students rotate and add sticky notes. Finish by naming the patterns together.

One-word whip around

Go around the circle and have each student give one word for how they're feeling or what they're taking with them. Fast, no pressure.

Anonymous cards

Have students answer on index cards, no names. Read a few out loud. Great for the harder questions.

Small group to big group

Put students in groups of three or four to talk it out. Each group agrees on ONE sentence that sums up their thinking and shares it with the room.

Digital board

Set up a shared board or form so quieter students can post their thinking. Put responses on screen and talk through them together.
Part 2

Lesson plan: turn inspiration into action

30 to 40 minutesGrades 9 to 12Any size groupAdvisor led

Goal

Your students leave with ONE personal change for the next 30 days and ONE action for your school or group, using the WOOP method. Personal first, then the school.

What you need

  • The WOOP worksheet (bottom of this page), one per student
  • Pens, sticky notes, chart paper
  • A notes list of every session or speaker from the event

What is WOOP?

WWISH
OOUTCOME
OOBSTACLE
PPLAN

WOOP was developed by psychologist Gabriele Oettingen. It works because it doesn't stop at the wish. It makes you look honestly at what's in the way, and plan for it before you hit it. It's simple enough for any grade, and it ends with one action, not ten.

3 MIN
OPEN

Check in

One-word whip around: what's one word for how you left the event?
"We're not here to talk about everything we learned. We're here to pick ONE thing and actually do something with it."
7 MIN
STEP 1

1Reflect on everything

Put the list of sessions and speakers up where everyone can see it. Give students two to three minutes of quiet writing, then a quick partner share.
  • What resonated with you?
  • What sat with you?
  • What left an imprint?
Then have each student circle the ONE thing that stuck with them most. That's what they'll use for their personal WOOP in Step 2.

Want more? Swap in two or three questions from the reflection questions above. Push past the first answer. It's usually the one they think an adult wants to hear.

8 MIN
STEP 2

2WOOP for you

Start personal. Using the ONE thing they circled, each student builds a WOOP for one change they'll make in the next 30 days. Go letter by letter out loud. Ask the question, give 60 to 90 seconds to write, hear one or two answers, move on.

W: Wish

What do you want to come true?

Big enough to matter, small enough to start.

"I want to stop saying yes to everything so I have time for me."

O: Outcome

What would it look and feel like if it happened?

Get specific. Picture it.

"I'd sleep more, stress less, and actually enjoy the things I lead."

O: Obstacle

What really gets in the way?

Look inside first. A habit, a feeling, a thought. Be honest.

"I feel guilty saying no. I don't want to let anyone down."

P: Plan

What's ONE action? Make it an if/then.

If the obstacle shows up, then I will...

"If someone asks me to take on something new, then I'll say: let me check and get back to you."
8 MIN
STEP 3

3Share and find the school focus

In groups of three or four, each student shares their ONE thing and why it stuck. Then each group answers: what's ONE issue or opportunity at our school that came up most? Groups share out with the whole room.

Tip: write each group's answer on chart paper. Pick one as a room, or let each group run with their own. That becomes the wish for Step 4.

10 MIN
STEP 4

4WOOP for the school or group

Same four questions, now as a team. Do this one together, out loud.
  • W: What do we want to improve or change? "Every student feels like they belong."
  • O: What would it look like if it happened? "Nobody eats lunch alone."
  • O: What gets in the way at our school? "We stick to our own friend groups."
  • P: What's ONE action we can take in the next 30 days? "Start a lunch buddy table."
4 MIN
CLOSE

Share the plan

Have each student read their personal plan out loud to a partner. Saying it to someone makes it real. Then post the school or group WOOP somewhere everyone will see it.
"Now the question is: what will you do with it?"

Keep the momentum

Debrief fast

Run this within 48 hours of any event, while it's still fresh.

Keep it visible

Post the WOOPs in your room. Put the school or group WOOP on your meeting agenda.

Check in

At 1 week and 30 days: What worked? What got in the way? What's the next step?

Facilitator tips

  • Keep everyone moving. Partners, small groups, standing up. Nobody needs to sit for 40 minutes.
  • Nobody has to share anything personal. Offer a pass every time.
  • If a group wants to change everything, ask: "If we could only do ONE, which one?"
  • Model it. Share your own WOOP first. It gives students permission to be honest.
  • Celebrate the small stuff at your check-ins. One action done beats ten planned.
