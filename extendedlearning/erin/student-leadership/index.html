Turn inspiration into action

The WOOP Method for Student Leadership

A ready-to-run lesson for advisors to lead with their student leadership group after any event. Built for a 30 to 40 minute class period. Adjust it to fit your time.

Overview

Goals and objectives

30 to 40 minutesGrades 9 to 12Advisor ledAny size group

Goal

Students will turn what they heard at an event into real action. They will reflect on what impacted them, build a personal plan for the next 30 days, and work together on a plan to improve their school or group.

Objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Reflect on the sessions and presenters they experienced and name the ONE takeaway that impacted them most.
  2. Use the WOOP method (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) to set a personal goal for the next 30 days.
  3. Connect their takeaway to an issue or opportunity at their school and agree on ONE focus as a group.
  4. Build a group action plan with clear steps, who is responsible, and a check-in date.

What you need

  • A list of every session, workshop, and speaker your students saw
  • The WOOP worksheet, one per student (download it here)
  • Pens, sticky notes, and chart paper
  • A spot to post the group's action plan

Quick info

  • Time: 30 to 40 minutes
  • Grades: 9 to 12
  • Group: Student leadership
  • Led by: Advisor

Lesson plan

Step by step

3 MIN
OPEN

Check in

Go around the room: what's one word for how you left the event?
"We're not here to talk about everything we learned. We're here to pick ONE thing and actually do something with it."
7 MIN
STEP 1

1Reflect on everything

Put the list of sessions and presenters where everyone can see it. Students can reflect any way that works for your group: writing, partners, small groups, or a gallery walk. The point is for students to share their thoughts, ideas, and opinions on each session and presenter, then land on the ONE thing that stuck with them most.
  • What resonated with you?
  • What sat with you?
  • What left an imprint?
By the end of this step: every student has named their ONE thing.

Push past the first answer. It's usually the one they think an adult wants to hear.

More reflection questions ↓ Ways to run it ↓
8 MIN
STEP 2

2WOOP for you

Using their ONE thing, each student builds a WOOP for one change they'll make in their personal life in the next 30 days. Read each question out loud and give them time to write.

W: Wish

Based on what stood out to you, what did it make you want to come true in your life?

Big enough to matter, small enough to start.

“I want to stop comparing myself to people online.”

O: Outcome

What would it look like if it happened? How would you feel?

Get specific. Picture it.

“I'd feel more confident and actually sleep instead of scrolling at night.”

O: Obstacle

What really gets in the way?

Look inside first. A habit, a feeling, a thought. Be honest.

“When I'm bored or stressed, I grab my phone without thinking.”

P: Plan

What's ONE action you'll take? Make it an if/then.

If my obstacle shows up, then I will...

“If I catch myself scrolling after 10, then I'll put my phone across the room and text a friend instead.”

Then share and reflect

Students share their ONE thing, why it stuck with them, and their plan. Pairs, small groups, or a few volunteers to the whole room. Whatever works for your group.
  • What's your ONE thing?
  • Why did it stick with you?
  • What's your plan?
Now switch: from personal to school
8 MIN
STEP 3

3Find the school focus

Based on their biggest takeaway, students look at their school. Each student shares their ONE thing and why it stuck, this time as it relates to their school. Then the group answers:
"What's ONE issue or opportunity at our school that came up most?"
Write the answers on chart paper. Pick one as a room, or let each group run with their own.
By the end of this step: your group has ONE school focus. That's the wish for Step 4.
10 MIN
STEP 4

4WOOP for the school, then make it an action plan

Same four questions, now as a team. Do this one together, out loud.
  • W: What do we want to improve or change? "Every student feels like they belong."
  • O: What would it look like if it happened? "Nobody eats lunch alone. New students know people by week two."
  • O: What gets in the way at our school? "We stick to our own friend groups and don't notice who's left out."
  • P: What's ONE action we'll take in the next 30 days? "Start a lunch buddy table."

Turn the plan into action

A plan without names and dates stays a wish. Fill this out together before anyone leaves.

Set a check-in date on the calendar before you close.
4 MIN
CLOSE

Say it out loud

Each student reads their personal plan to a partner. Saying it to someone makes it real. Post the school action plan where everyone will see it.
"Now the question is: what will you do with it?"

Keep the momentum

Debrief fast

Run this within 48 hours of any event, while it's still fresh.

Keep it visible

Post the WOOPs in your room. Put the action plan on your meeting agenda.

Check in

At 1 week and 30 days: What worked? What got in the way? What's the next step?

Facilitator tips

  • Keep everyone moving. Partners, small groups, standing up. Nobody needs to sit for 40 minutes.
  • Nobody has to share anything personal. Offer a pass every time.
  • If a group wants to change everything, ask: "If we could only do ONE, which one?"
  • Model it. Share your own WOOP first. It gives students permission to be honest.
  • Celebrate the small stuff at your check-ins. One action done beats ten planned.
Question bank

Reflection questions

Use these with Step 1 to swap in or go deeper. Pick two or three, not all of them. You can also use them on their own anytime, like a quick meeting opener.

Start with them

Ask your students:

  • What from this event is still sitting with you?
  • What left an imprint on you? Not your favorite part, the part you keep thinking about.
  • What's your black dot right now? The thing you can't stop staring at.
  • What part of it is in your control? What part isn't?
  • Where's your white space? Where can you still show up?
  • What do you need to stop carrying?
  • When was the last time you did something just to refill your own cup?

Then the school or group

Ask your students:

  • What's one thing you wish was different about our school?
  • Who in our school might feel unseen right now?
  • What's one thing our group does well that we should do more of?
  • What gets in the way of us following through?
  • If you could change ONE thing in the next three months, what would it be?
  • What would students say about our group if we weren't in the room?

About the event

Ask your students:

  • Which session or speaker is still in your head? Why that one?
  • What's one idea you heard that could actually work here?
  • Did anything challenge you or make you uncomfortable? What was it?
  • What's something you want to try in the next 30 days?

Going deeper

For when your group is ready:

  • What's the story you tell yourself when things get hard? Is it true?
  • What's something you've had to accept that made you stronger?
  • What are you grateful for right now, even in the middle of something hard?
  • What kind of leader do you want people to remember you as?

Ways to run it

Pick a format that fits your group. Some students think out loud, some need quiet first. Mix it up.

Quiet journal

Give students two to three minutes of silent writing before anyone talks. That way everyone has something to say when it's time to share.

Turn and talk

Pair students up, one minute each. Then call on a few pairs to share what their partner said, not what they said.

Four corners

Label the corners Impactful, Challenge, Growth, Curiosity. Have students walk to the one that fits what stuck with them, then talk with their corner.

Sticky note gallery walk

Post questions on chart paper around the room. Have students rotate and add sticky notes. Finish by naming the patterns together.

One-word whip around

Go around the circle and have each student give one word for how they're feeling or what they're taking with them. Fast, no pressure.

Anonymous cards

Have students answer on index cards, no names. Read a few out loud. Great for the harder questions.

Small group to big group

Put students in groups of three or four to talk it out. Each group agrees on ONE sentence that sums up their thinking and shares it with the room.

Digital board

Set up a shared board or form so quieter students can post their thinking. Put responses on screen and talk through them together.
